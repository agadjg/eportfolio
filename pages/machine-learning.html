From Industry 4.0 to 5.0: Toward a resilient, human-centric paradigm

The MGM ransomware attack highlights that although Industry 4.0 champions efficiency gains through automation and interconnectivity, these measures alone are insufficient. Industry 5.0 promotes an advanced ecosystem spanning technology, data integrity, and operational reliability, accompanied by human-critical oversight, sound judgment, and ongoing feedback. Implementing these practices enables well-informed decision-making, safeguards enterprises and human well-being, and builds societal trust in the underlying system.

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

In the realm of big data, where vast volumes and complex structures can obscure meaningful patterns and introduce data quality issues, EDA becomes essential. It enables analysts to gain a thorough understanding of their datasets, assess data quality, uncover hidden patterns, identify anomalies, and formulate hypotheses. EDA is not a checklist but an iterative process in which initial observations determine what needs to be investigated further. It helps develop key questions and assumptions to discuss with stakeholders before moving to feature selection and machine learning, supporting informed business decisions.

Artificial Neural Networks: From Simple to Multilayer Perceptor

Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) mimic how the brain’s neurons process signals, adjust weights, and learn to make predictions. I explored fundamental concepts from perceptrons to multilayer networks, including activation functions, error calculation, weight updates, and backpropagation. Exercises demonstrated how hidden layers allow ANNs to learn complex nonlinear patterns and highlighted key architectural choices. I applied these principles to Fashion-MNIST with a multilayer perceptron. Differences between training, validation, and test accuracy revealed overfitting and showed why training accuracy alone is inadequate for model evaluation.
