From Industry 4.0 to 5.0: Toward a resilient, human-centric paradigm

The MGM ransomware attack highlights that while Industry 4.0 champions efficiency gains through automation and interconnectivity, these advances alone are insufficient. From a data integrity perspective, companies must ensure their data remains trustworthy before resuming operations, restarting personalised services, and building machine learning predictions. Artificial Intelligence (AI) can amplify data inaccuracies, causing substantial business risk. Industry 5.0 promotes an advanced ecosystem that integrates technology, data integrity, and operational reliability, with essential human oversight, sound judgment, and ongoing feedback. Adopting these practices supports informed decision-making, safeguards enterprises and individuals, and builds public trust in the underlying system.

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

In the realm of big data, where vast volumes and complex structures can obscure meaningful patterns and introduce data quality issues, EDA becomes essential. It helps EDA enables analysts to understand datasets, evaluate data quality, discover hidden patterns, identify anomalies, and generate hypotheses. EDA is not a checklist but an iterative and progressive process. Each finding informs the next step and direction of analysis. New observations continually guide what to investigate next. Additionally, EDA supports the development of key questions for stakeholder discussions and helps challenge assumptions before advancing to feature selection and machine learning, ultimately supporting more informed business decisions.

From Correlation to Regression Models

Exploratory data analysis (EDA) is closely linked to predictive modelling. Identifying and modelling relationships between variables helps inform model selection. Correlation measures the strength and direction of associations but does not indicate causation. A strong statistical relationship alone cannot prove that changes in one variable cause changes in another. This distinction is crucial during EDA, where correlation can reveal potential predictors, redundant variables, and multicollinearity, yet these findings must always be interpreted within the problem’s context. Linear, multiple linear, and polynomial regression model these relationships, generate predictions, and assess how well the models represent the underlying data.

Supervised Learning: Linear Regression

Understanding the progression from exploring relationships to modelling is essential. EDA identifies potential relationships, correlation quantifies their strength and direction, whereas linear regression extends this by modelling relationships between predictors and the target variable. As a supervised learning technique, linear regression fits a line that minimises the sum of squared residuals between actual and predicted values for a continuous target. However, model evaluation and residual analysis demonstrate that performance metrics alone do not provide a complete picture. Examining residuals helps determine whether prediction errors are randomly distributed or if the model has missed underlying patterns. Applying this to both Population–GDP and vehicle data helped connect the statistical concepts with practical model fitting, prediction and evaluation.

Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) mimic how the brain’s neurons process signals, adjust weights, and learn to make predictions. I explored fundamental concepts from perceptrons to multilayer networks, including activation functions, error calculation, weight updates, and backpropagation.
